Tryout Week
Performance Checklist

Three nights out to after the ice. Tap each line when it's done, your phone remembers it. Same list every tryout, every camp.

From Kenny

Tryouts aren't won at tryouts. They're won in the three days before, by the kid who shows up slept, fed, hydrated, and already warm when the others are still lacing up. None of this is complicated. The edge is that almost nobody does all of it.

One rule for the whole week: nothing new. No new foods, no new gear, no new routine. Tryout week is for doing the known things perfectly.

Kenny, Prodigy Athletics

3 nights out

This is where the week starts. Everything from here forward is about arriving rested and fueled, and sleep is the one you can't make up later.

Sleep

✓
9.5 hours of sleep tonight. 10 or more is ideal.Count back from wake-up time and set the bedtime now. That's the number, not "go to bed early."

Screen time

✓
No screens in the hour before bed.
✓
Under 4 hours of total screen time today.

Water

✓
Minimum 3 litres today.Sip it through the day. Cramming it at night just wrecks the sleep.

Food

✓
Three real meals, nothing new.Familiar food, normal portions. This week is not the week to try anything.

2 nights out

Same standards as last night, fully locked in. No slipping now.

Sleep

✓
9.5 hours again. 10 or more is ideal.

Screen time

✓
No screens in the hour before bed.
✓
Under 4 hours of total screen time today.

Water

✓
Minimum 3 litres today.

Logistics

✓
Confirm the ice times, the rink, and how long the drive takes.Know when you're leaving the house. Rushing on the day burns energy you want on the ice.
✓
Quick gear check: anything broken, missing, or needing a sharpen gets handled today, not tomorrow night.

1 night out

The most important night of the week. Tonight's job is simple: eat well, pack the bag, and get to bed on time.

Sleep

✓
9.5 hours minimum. This is the one that shows up on the ice tomorrow.Bedtime set. Alarm set with time to wake up properly, eat, and move before leaving.

Screen time

✓
No screens in the hour before bed. Phone charges outside the bedroom tonight.
✓
Under 4 hours of total screen time today.

Water

✓
Minimum 3 litres today.

Nutrition

✓
A full, hearty, well balanced dinner.A real protein (chicken, turkey, beef, fish), a starch (pasta, rice, potatoes, quinoa), vegetables, and normal fats. Familiar food, good portion. This is the fuel for tomorrow.
✓
Tomorrow's snacks packed tonight: before the ice, between skates, and after.See "Between skates" below for what goes in the bag by how much time there is.

Bag packed

✓
Skates, sharpened, laces checked. Spare laces in the bag.
✓
Helmet and cage, shoulder pads, elbow pads, gloves, pants, shin pads, jock, neck guard, mouthguard.
✓
Jersey and socks (both colours if they gave you two), plus anything the tryout told you to bring.
✓
Sticks taped, and a spare stick. Fresh tape roll in the bag.
✓
Your own water bottle, filled. A second one if there's more than one ice time.
✓
Off ice warm-up kit: running shoes, shorts or track pants, a t-shirt, and a layer for the rink.
✓
Snacks and shaker bottle packed (see the gap guide below).
✓
Towel, skate guards, any registration or paperwork, and a small first aid bit (tape, a bandage).
✓
Clothes for the morning laid out. Bag by the door.

Day of

Wake up with time to spare. Water first, then food, then move. Walk into the rink already warm.

Morning

✓
Up with time to spare. Big glass of water before anything else.
✓
A familiar breakfast: mostly carbs, some protein, keep fats light.Oats or toast with honey or jam, fruit, eggs or yogurt. Aim for 2 to 3 hours before the ice if the schedule allows. Never skip it.
✓
Morning mobility, 10 to 15 minutes. The routine is below.

Morning mobility

Two rounds through. Smooth and easy, this is to wake the body up and loosen stiffness, not to stretch hard.

  1. Dynamic hip flexor stretch each side
  2. Dynamic frog stretch
  3. Plate tea cup (shoulder mobility) 6 each side, a small plate or water bottle works
  4. Dynamic pigeon stretch each side
  5. Wide leg seated forward fold
  6. Superband dislocates 6 reps, a towel works if you have no band
  7. T-spine rotation with reach each side
  8. Side-lying IT band stretch each side
  9. World's greatest stretch 5 each side
  10. Seated hip twist 5 each side

Before the ice

✓
At the rink early enough to warm up properly, roughly 45 minutes before the ice.
✓
Off ice warm-up done, in running shoes, 20 to 30 minutes before stepping on. The routine is below.
✓
Water bottle full and in the player's box. Sipping, not chugging.

Off ice warm-up

One round through, in order. Find a hallway or a spot outside the dressing room with about 10 metres of room. Build the pace up as you go, the last three should feel fast.

  1. Shuttle run 10 m
  2. High knee skipping 10 m
  3. Runner stretch 4 each side
  4. Inchworm walk 5
  5. Downward dog toe tap to push-up 4 each side
  6. 90-90 hip switch to lift and reach 4 each side
  7. Push-up to T-rotation 3 each side
  8. Pogo hops 10
  9. Single leg lateral pogo hops 5 each leg
  10. Glute bridge 5, 2 second hold at the top
  11. Double broad jump 3
  12. Backpedal to sprint 2 reps, 5 m back then 10 m forward

Right after each skate

✓
Post skate cool-down, about 10 minutes, before the gear even comes all the way off if you can. The routine is below.
✓
Water, and the right snack for how long until the next skate (next section).

Post skate cool-down

Two rounds through. Keep every stretch gentle. The goal is to stay loose and let the tissue recover, not to push range.

  1. Cat-cow 4
  2. Thread the needle 3 each side
  3. Dynamic hip flexor stretch each side
  4. Dynamic pigeon stretch each side
  5. Dynamic rear foot elevated hip flexor stretch each side
  6. Side-lying IT band stretch each side
  7. Dynamic frog stretch
  8. Straddle side stretch, alternating 3 each side, easy

Between skates

More than one ice time in a day? What they eat between skates is decided by one thing: how long until the next one. On high activity days, fats take a back seat until the evening.

Under 2 hours

A little easy digesting protein and a light carb, keep fats low. Whey or your protein powder in water, Fairlife, or skim milk. A banana or a few dates alongside. Small, not a meal.

2 to 4 hours

Carbs first, plus some protein. Fresh fruit (pears, bananas, oranges), dried fruit (dates, raisins, apricots), bread or toast with honey or jam, and a protein source like Fairlife, yogurt, or a shake. Then at about 2 hours out, the small protein and carb snack from above.

4 hours or more

A full meal. Chicken or turkey, a starch like pasta, rice, or quinoa, and vegetables. Keep fats low for now. Then follow the 2 hour snack above as the next skate gets close.

✓
Know the gap between skates and pack for it, the night before.
✓
Refill the bottle after every skate. The 3 litres still applies, more on a double day.
✓
Feet up between skates. Shade, a quiet spot, gear off. Save the energy for the ice, not the lobby.

After the ice

Skating is done for the day, so now it's all recovery and no digestion to worry about. This is where most of the day's calories live.

✓
Cool-down done (routine above).
✓
The big meal: carbs, protein, and fats all together.Fats come back in here. Fish, avocado, a cream based sauce on the pasta, whatever they like. Get a lot of good food in.
✓
Keep drinking water through the evening.
✓
If there's another skate tomorrow, tonight is a "night before" again: 9.5 hours, no screens for the last hour, bag repacked.
The pattern for the whole week, in one line: sleep 9.5 plus, 3 litres of water, screens off an hour before bed, nothing new, warm before the ice, cool down after, fuel by the gap, and eat big once the skating is done.
