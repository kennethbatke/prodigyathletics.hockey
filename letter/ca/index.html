Build the Leader.
The Athlete Will Follow.

A letter from Kenny, founder of Prodigy Athletics

A 10 minute read. A 2 minute decision.

Hey, Kenny here.

I work with a small number of committed hockey families, and this page is everything I would tell you on a call. What I actually do, who it works for, what it costs, and how to know if your player is one of ours. Nothing held back, no call required.

Before any of that, take a second and ask yourself how you honestly feel about your player's development right now.

For most parents who find me, it sounds something like this:

"My child has the tools. Everyone sees it. Something is holding them back."
"They're a different player in practice than they are in games."
"One bad shift ruins the whole night."
"We feel like we're doing everything we can, but it still feels like something is off or missing."
"They know what they should be doing. I'm not the one who can get them to do it anymore."
"It's getting exhausting not feeling like I can talk to my own child after the game anymore. I'm tired of feeling that tension."
"We both see their confidence slipping, but no matter what I say my words fall on deaf ears."

If a few of those hit home, keep reading. And before anything else, I want to be clear about something.

Your son or daughter isn't soft, lazy, unmotivated, or broken. The problem is the standard model of hockey development has only ever focused on hockey.

Think about what your family is buying right now. An hour of skills here. A week of camp there. Advisors, academies, the works. Each of these is focused on what your player can do and where they may go in the future. None of it focuses on who they're becoming along the way.

But games aren't won and lost only on the ice anymore. They're fought on fronts outside the arena. Sleep. Food. Training. Recovery. Travel. Time and stress management. The voice in their head after a mistake. Nobody is preparing that part of your player's week, but everyone is expecting they know it. And that part is what actually decides how far they go.

That's the part I coach.

Confidence Is Not a Personality Trait

Most people think some players are born with confidence and some aren't. I've coached enough athletes across North America and Europe to tell you that's simply not true.

Like any skill, confidence is learned. It's built with a combination of evidence and mental tools, merging habits and mindset in a reinforcing loop until they trust themselves under pressure because they've earned that trust. You can't talk a player into it, buy it in a weekend clinic, or download it from a YouTube video. It gets built, brick by brick, with proper structure, support, and accountability toward clear and ambitious goals.

If it hasn't registered yet, here's what matters most to me, and it's the part most programs never touch.

This was never just a hockey play. It's a life play that runs through hockey.

The habits that make a 15 year old dangerous on the ice: owning their sleep, fuelling their body, training with intent, resetting after failure, communicating productively with a coach, and advocating for themselves and their goals, are the exact same habits that make a 25 year old dangerous in whatever they choose to do next.

Your player doesn't need to see that yet. They just want to make the next team, and that's perfect. We use the goal they care about to build the person you care about. We give them the skills and the reps now, so when the opportunity comes, they're ready for it. On the ice and everywhere after it.

Who You'd Be Trusting With Your Player

I'm a former junior and university hockey player. I developed late, battled injuries, faced politics, wanted to quit, and carved my path forward with structure, belief, and relentless effort. I never had a mentor or a roadmap. I had to build one the hard way, and I remember the exact feeling of being the player everyone was unsure about.

In university, I finally got that roadmap, and I developed more in the last four years of my career than in any other period. It felt wrong that I received the support and resources I needed AFTER achieving my goal of getting a scholarship.

That disconnect is what birthed Prodigy. Today I mentor players across North America and Europe. I'm the source of all the coaching, and there are no assistant coaches you get passed off to after you join. This isn't an academy with my name on the door and someone else doing the work. When your family works with Prodigy, you work with me.

How It Works: Built From the Inside Out

When a player joins, the first thing we build is what I call the textbook week. The 100% version of their life: sleep locked in, meals planned around training, strength work placed around their ice times, skills, school, recovery, all of it laid out hour by hour. Then we work backwards from the textbook to their real life and get the two as close together as we can.

From there, we work on four fronts:

Training That Transfers

Personalized strength, speed, and conditioning built around your player's actual schedule and physical demands, adjusted based on feedback and fatigue, both in-season and off-season.

Fueling and Recovery

A custom nutrition plan that's easy to follow, sleep standards, and structure that prevents burnout instead of causing it.

Confidence and Leadership

Addressing the root of inconsistency, staying calm under pressure, resetting after mistakes, and handling the tough conversations to progress your player beyond skill.

On Ice IQ

Game breakdowns and skill analysis, so the rest of the work they're putting in shows up where it matters most to them: in games.

And the piece parents tell me changes their house the most: the accountability comes from me now, not from you. You get to go back to being the parent. The one who watches, supports, and enjoys the games. I'll hold the standard.

Read This Part Carefully

I'd rather lose your enrollment than take it and fail your player. So let me be straight about who this doesn't work for.

This won't work for your family if:

✕

Your player believes their goals will happen without work. The most common dealbreaker isn't a lack of desire, it's a player who is unwilling to confront the effort their goals require, and isn't willing to change that belief. Until that belief is open to change, added coaching is a waste.

✕

You're looking for a quick fix with "just a couple sessions" or a miracle right before tryouts. We start with a 12-week commitment, and the compounding is the whole point. If that level of commitment is off-putting, the solution you're looking for doesn't live here.

✕

You want a coach who tells your player what they want to hear. I care about my athletes too much to lie to them. Of course I want the best for them, but if their commitment doesn't match their goals, it's my responsibility to close that gap.

✕

You believe talent and politics decide who makes it. If the closest influence in their life believes their situation is outside their control, no coach is going to fix that.

✕

You need to sit between your player and their coach. The athlete owns the relationship with me, and that ownership is where the growth comes from. Parents are a great extra set of eyes and ears, but the reason you bring in a coach is to relax into being a parent again.

But if:

✓

Your player says they want this, even if their habits haven't caught up to their ambition yet.

✓

You believe character is built, not born.

✓

You can hand your player the keys and let me hold the standard. In other words, you're willing to trust your player to follow through, and willing to let them surprise you.

✓

You care as much about who they're becoming as where they play next season.

Then keep reading, because this was built for your family.

Questions You're Probably Asking

"What if my player starts hot and fades after a few weeks?"

That's not a risk to the program. That IS the program. Motivation fades for every athlete on earth. The difference here is that when it fades, there's a plan, a standard, and a coach who notices and reaches out. Consistency when they don't feel like it is one of the skills we're building.

"How much time does this take? Their schedule is already packed."

The plan is built around your player's real life: their school, their team schedule, their family commitments. Most families find it replaces chaos with structure rather than adding load. If the week doesn't fit, we adjust the plan, not your family.

"Is my player old enough? Good enough?"

I work directly with committed players 13 and up, from players fighting for a AA spot to junior and college athletes. Skill level matters far less than desire and maturity. If your player is younger, take the 90 second assessment here and it will route you to the right starting point.

"We already have training, a trainer, or an advisor in place. Can we get just pieces of the program?"

No, and it's not because I want to sell you more. The results come from the pieces working together as one system. Cherry-picking pieces is how families end up with a drawer full of programs that didn't stick. If your player already has people they love in their corner, keep them. I build around what's working instead of tearing it out, and families are often surprised to find their trainer or advisor becomes more valuable once the pieces are finally connected. But I coach the whole athlete or not at all. If a la carte is what you're looking for, we see development differently, and that's okay.

"What if I'm still not sure?"

Usually the families who are unsure just haven't seen enough results from real people. Read through our results page or check out our reviews on Facebook.

"I have a few questions I still want answered before I get started. Where do I ask them?"

If you're still uncertain, fire me an email at kenny.batke@prodigyathletics.ca, or book a free fit call. I can answer any of your questions and give you a straight read on whether the program is right for you or whether you'd be wasting your time and money.
